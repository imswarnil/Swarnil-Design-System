Design tokens are a contract, not a colour list

A token layer earns its keep the day the brand changes and nothing in the components has to move.

14 Sep 2026 11 min read Design systems

Every design system arrives at the same argument eventually, and it is never really about colour. Somebody wants a button that is a slightly different blue, and the honest answer takes forty minutes and never quite lands.

Two tiers, and the rule between them

The palette is tier one: eleven steps of neutral, eight hues, and a shared lightness ladder so any two of them at the same step carry the same visual weight. Nothing reads from it directly.

Tier two is the argument. --fg-muted, --bg-raised, --line-subtle — names that say what a value is for rather than what it is.

A component that reads --ink-700 has hard-coded an appearance. A component that reads --fg-muted has asked for a job, and the theme answers it.

What that buys you

  • A dark theme that is one declaration per token, not a second stylesheet.
  • A rebrand that is six lines — the accent and its five relatives.
  • A component library with no colours in it at all.

Counting the cost

The overhead is real and it is small: one indirection at read time, and a naming discipline enforced by something other than good intentions.

Where it usually breaks

Not in the tokens. In the exceptions — the one card that needed a border just slightly stronger, shipped as a hex at 5pm on a Friday.
