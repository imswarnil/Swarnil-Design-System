The long version

I did not set out to make videos. I set out to stop explaining the same thing for the fourth time. A colleague asked how a dataflow worked, I recorded my screen instead of booking a meeting, and eleven people watched it. Two of them were me, on another browser, checking the audio.

What I learned in the ten years since is that the explaining is the work. Not a by-product of it — the actual work. Writing a tutorial forces you to find the joint in a problem where it actually bends, and half the time you discover you never understood it; you had just memorised the shape of the thing you typed last.

The thing you cannot explain in twenty minutes is usually the thing you have not finished learning.

So this site is a working notebook. The code is here because I needed it, the videos are here because making them taught me the code, and the courses are here because the videos kept getting comments that deserved more than a reply.

The design system underneath all of it exists for the least romantic reason imaginable: I had six sites that looked like six people made them, and I was tired of deciding what a button was.

The path

Ten years, five turns

Nothing here was a plan. Each step was the obvious next thing, which is the only kind of plan that has ever worked for me.

  1. 2016

  2. 2018

    Consulting by day, filming at night. The videos got better because the day job kept giving me things worth explaining.

  3. 2020

    CRM Analytics, from zero. Four hundred students in the first month and a very steep lesson in what "prerequisites" means.

  4. 2023

    Went on the road

    A camera, one lens, and twenty-three countries. The travel blog started as a way to remember which town the footage came from.

  5. 2026

    One system for all of it

    Six sites, three themes and a YouTube channel that all looked like different people made them. So I stopped and built the design system first.

How I work

One episode, five steps

Every video on this site went through exactly this, in exactly this order. The order is the part that took me six years to get right.

  1. Build the thing firstNo script until it works. A tutorial written before the code is a tutorial that lies about the hard parts.
  2. Write the one sentenceIf I cannot say what the video is in one line, the video is two videos and I have not noticed yet.
  3. Shoot the endingFilmed first, always. Knowing where it lands keeps the middle from wandering.
  4. Cut the first ten minutesThe setup is never as interesting as I thought while I was filming it.
  5. Publish, then write it downThe blog post is the video's transcript with the mistakes left in. That is the useful half.
