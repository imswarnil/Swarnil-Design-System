Joins, and where they go wrong

What this lesson is really about

A join is not a technique, it is a claim about the world: that one row over here corresponds to one row over there. Almost every broken dashboard I have been called in to fix was a dashboard where that claim was false and nobody had said it out loud.

So this lesson is mostly about grain — what one row means — and only incidentally about the join syntax.

Before you start

Open opportunity_lines.csv from the project files. The whole lesson works on it, and it is deliberately dirty.

The fan-out, in one picture

When you join a table at the order grain to one at the line-item grain, every order-level number is now repeated once per line. Sum it and you have invented revenue.

The fix

Aggregate to the grain you actually want before the join, not after it. Two extra steps in the recipe, and the number stops moving every time someone adds a line.

SAQL
-- aggregate first, then join
q = load "opportunity_lines";
q = group q by 'opportunity_id';
q = foreach q generate 'opportunity_id', sum('amount') as 'lines_total';
